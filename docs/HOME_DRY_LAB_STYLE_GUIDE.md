# Home Dry Lab Style Guide

## Voice
Write like a helpful Australian research site. The voice should sound like a practical person who has compared the evidence, spotted the trade-offs, and wants to make the reader's decision easier.

## What Good Sounds Like
- Direct answer early
- Clear trade-offs
- Honest uncertainty
- Natural Australian phrasing
- Useful buyer guidance
- Calm, grounded wording

## What Bad Sounds Like
- Generic affiliate hype
- Corporate filler
- Fake authority
- Overconfident health claims
- Repetitive AI transitions
- Empty feature lists with no decision help

## Preferred Wording
- power bill
- running cost
- laundry
- buyer feedback patterns
- retailer listings
- based on published specs
- not confirmed
- who should avoid it

## Non-Tested Product Wording
When a product has not been personally tested, prefer phrases like:
- Based on manufacturer specifications, Australian retailer listings and repeated buyer feedback patterns
- Buyer reviews suggest
- Published specs list
- Retailer information shows
- We found repeated complaints about

## Reader Standard
Each article should pass these checks:
- Would this help an Australian homeowner or renter make a better decision?
- Does it say something competitors do not?
- Does it explain downsides honestly?
- Does it avoid pretending Home Dry Lab tested the product?
- Does it sound like a real person wrote it?
